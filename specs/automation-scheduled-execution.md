# Enable daily task automations with durable scheduled execution

- Status: Implementation in progress
- Catalog capability: household-automation
- Date: 2026-11-21
- Owners: Domus maintainers
- Scope: Complete daily single-task scheduled execution, scoped grants, recovery and timing adoption
- Related issue: https://github.com/vypdev/domus/issues/155
- Required review gates: product UX, architecture, testing, documentation, security/operations
- Open decisions blocking readiness: none; actual owner decisions are recorded in issue155

## 1. Executive summary

An authorized household member can deliberately enable a saved Daily task automation after reviewing its immutable revision, schedule, and narrowly scoped execution grant. Domus then admits eligible daily triggers durably and creates the proposed task through the existing task command path, without an open browser or interactive human session. Members can see the enabled revision, approved timing policy, upcoming times, current status, and inspectable outcomes for each scheduled run. A false stock condition records a no-task outcome. Startup skips daily times that became due while Domus was stopped and were not durably admitted, then continues at the next future time.

```text
Review a saved Daily revision, timing policy, and scoped grant -> explicitly enable -> durably admit eligible future triggers -> inspect each task, no-task, suppression, or attention outcome
```

## 2. Problem, current behavior, and evidence

### 2.1 Problem

Households can save an inactive Daily task proposal and preview its next times, but cannot arrange for Domus to execute it each day. The requested increment closes that gap with durable admission, scoped authority, recoverable outcomes, and explicit handling of timing changes and missed startup triggers.

### 2.2 Current behavior

The roadmap identifies daily schedule preview and inactive daily drafts as existing M4 work, while scheduled admission remains open (`docs/roadmap.md`). The retained owner specifies Daily drafts as inactive and says saving one does not create execution authority (`specs/household-automation.md`, “Save inactive daily automation drafts”). The accepted single-engine direction requires simple and advanced rules to share a versioned declarative model and durable engine, with run receipts, pinned revisions, current permission checks, and cancellation semantics (`docs/adr/0005-one-automation-engine.md`).

This delta adds explicit enablement and durable daily admission to that retained contract. On startup, skip only daily triggers that became due while Domus was stopped and had not already been durably admitted; persist an inspectable suppression/cursor fact and continue at the strict next-future time. Ordinary in-process worker delay does not itself suppress every due trigger. Already admitted work and committed receipts retain their original outcomes; cancelled work is not automatically resumed or replayed.

### 2.3 Evidence

- The roadmap lists daily preview and inactive drafts as present, and scheduled admission as open (`docs/roadmap.md`). The retained owner describes the preview, immutable inactive drafts, and existing manual execution boundaries (`specs/household-automation.md`).
- The single-engine architecture and disablement behavior are recorded in `docs/adr/0005-one-automation-engine.md`.
- Startup missed-trigger handling is an explicit product decision supplied with this issue: skip missed unadmitted daily times and continue with the next future time. Qualify its cutoff, worker races, disable/re-enable, and restoration behavior.
- The exact principal lifecycle and timing-policy adoption requirements are specified in this delta. Their implementation and verification evidence do not yet exist in the selected source.

### 2.4 Retrospective classification

Not applicable: this is a prospective specification delta, not a reconstruction of an implemented baseline.

## 3. Actors, surfaces, and terminology

An authorized household member reviews and enables a saved Daily revision, manages its grant and control, and inspects scheduled run history. The scheduled worker admits eligible triggers and invokes the ordinary task-creation use case under a Domus-owned technical rule principal. Household settings provide the proposed time-zone and recurrence policy; the rule retains its reviewed pinned timing policy until explicit adoption.

A **Daily revision** is an immutable saved task proposal with one local daily time and the existing optional stock condition. A **rule grant** is a non-human Domus authority owned by the household rule and bound to its installation/channel, household, rule, and explicitly enabled immutable revision. A **trigger** is one eligible occurrence of that rule's daily time. An **admitted run** is durable work already accepted for a trigger; its timing, snapshots, receipts, and outcome remain tied to that admission. A **suppression** records that a trigger was intentionally not admitted, including a missed startup trigger. A **timing policy** is the reviewed pinned time zone and recurrence-resolution policy used to resolve daily occurrences.

## 4. Goals, non-goals, and fixed invariants

### 4.1 Goals

- `SDD-I155-U3-R1` — Each enabled rule has a Domus-owned non-human grant bound to its installation/channel, household, rule, and explicitly enabled immutable revision. The grant remains rule-owned if the activating person leaves or loses permissions.
- `SDD-I155-U3-R2` — Grant management requires current automation-administration permission. A task-creation grant also requires current task-creation permission and verified household access. It delegates only task creation and necessary scoped condition reads; it conveys no global administrator authority, stored credentials, or fabricated `HumanIdentity`.
- `SDD-I155-U3-R3` — Deliberately reviewed enablement creates the grant. Recheck its binding and capability before admission and each effect. Revocation or disablement prevents new effects while preserving already-started atomic commands and their actual outcomes and history.
- `SDD-I155-U3-R4` — Saving a draft leaves the current grant unchanged. Explicitly switching the enabled revision retires its old binding and requires a deliberately granted new binding. Invalid household, installation, or channel bindings prevent execution.
- `SDD-I155-U3-R5` — Restart and restoration revalidate the persisted grant and binding, retain valid rule-owned identity, and preserve receipt/cursor provenance without redelivery or automatic resumption of cancelled work.
- `SDD-I155-U4-R1` — Continue future execution under the reviewed pinned time zone and recurrence-resolution policy until an authorized member explicitly reviews and adopts a new policy. A household timing change alone neither switches nor pauses an otherwise resolvable approved schedule.
- `SDD-I155-U4-R2` — Show the approved and proposed household policies and their upcoming resolved times, including relevant daylight-saving gaps and repeated-time differences. Adoption confirms the current rule/control revision and newly reviewed policy.
- `SDD-I155-U4-R3` — Keep admitted runs bound to their original timing, snapshots, and outcomes. Adoption affects future eligible triggers only; it does not rewrite prior work or replay missed or cancelled triggers.
- `SDD-I155-U4-R4` — If the approved policy cannot be verified or resolved, show an unavailable or review state without silently substituting the proposed household policy.

### 4.2 Non-goals

This increment does not add event triggers, additional action types, multi-step, wait, or parallel execution, or the optional advanced editor. It does not complete all M4 or first-release roadmap obligations. It does not alter the retained manual controls, runs, history, exact original-request recovery, or inactive-draft contract. Recurring-template editing and location management remain separate work.

### 4.3 Fixed product/safety invariants

- Scheduled execution uses the shared automation engine and the currently authorized task-creation use case, preserving task validation, receipts, audit, and outbox behavior. A scheduled invocation is not a manual trigger and never fabricates an authenticated human identity or borrows a member's administrator authority.
- Check current authority, grant binding, household scope, and enabled control before each admission and effect. Serialize disablement against admission and subsequent effects. An already-started atomic command may finish, and its real receipt and history remain visible.
- Saving another draft does not arm or alter execution. Switching the enabled immutable revision requires retiring the old binding and deliberate enablement of a new grant. A household, installation, or channel change that invalidates the binding prevents execution.
- Persist trigger identity, pinned rule/timing/control facts, eligible-time cursor, admission, and run/step outcomes so retries, concurrent workers, lost replies, restart, and restore cannot duplicate effects. False stock conditions produce an inspectable no-task result. Re-enabling permits future work without resuming the cancelled queue.
- On startup, suppress due daily triggers that were not durably admitted before Domus stopped, record a bounded inspectable suppression/cursor fact, and continue strictly at the next future time. Do not backfill missed dates or reinterpret suppressed triggers after restart, another worker, disable/re-enable, or restoration. This rule does not drop admitted work or committed receipts and does not turn ordinary in-process worker delay into blanket suppression.
- Keep the reviewed timing policy pinned across household timing changes. Adoption is an explicit authorized review and confirmation; it changes future eligible triggers only. An unverifiable or unresolvable approved policy is surfaced for review and never silently replaced.
- Keep current manual controls, history, and original-request recovery independently available. Show scheduled versus manual invocation clearly, distinguish task success from failed subsequent reads, and distinguish unavailable history from an empty history. Preserve input and focus during recoverable errors and explicit conflict review; provide accessible English and Spanish behavior for keyboard and 390 px touch layouts, both themes and compositions, using the exact published `ha-ui` version consumed by Domus.

## 5. Current versus proposed product journey

The retained owner in `specs/household-automation.md` defines the shared automation engine, immutable rule revisions, manual controls, and existing task proposal. This companion adds durable daily scheduled execution and timing-policy adoption; it does not change those retained contracts.

Today, a saved Daily definition is an inactive draft. The current manual execution path in `backend/src/domus/application/automation_execution.py` accepts positively recognized manual revisions and a current human identity; `backend/src/domus/application/automation_schedule_preview.py` previews upcoming daily times using the household’s current time policy. There is no daily scheduler, admission lifecycle, or rule-owned technical grant established by these files. The domain accepts distinct Manual and Daily forms in `backend/src/domus/domain/automation.py`.

Proposed journey: an authorized member reviews a saved Daily revision, its next resolved times, and the narrow task-creation grant, then deliberately enables that immutable revision. Domus records the rule-owned grant and durable schedule binding. At each eligible daily time, the shared engine admits one run, evaluates the optional stock condition, and either records a no-task result or invokes the ordinary authorized task command. The member can inspect scheduled status and outcomes, disable future work, or review and explicitly adopt a changed household timing policy. Saving a draft alone does not enable it or alter the active grant. The rule remains owned by the household rule if its activating member leaves or loses permission.

For timing changes, the reviewed time zone and recurrence-resolution policy remain pinned and continue to govern otherwise resolvable future work. The review surface shows the approved policy and proposed household policy with their upcoming resolved times and relevant gap or repeated-time differences. Adoption requires an authorized member to confirm the current rule/control revision and the newly reviewed policy; it affects future eligible triggers only.

These journeys are exercised by the rule-grant ownership, authorization, enablement/revocation, draft/revision-switch, and restart/restoration behaviors declared as `inventory3-C01`, `inventory3-C02`, `inventory3-C03`, `inventory3-C04`, and `inventory3-C05`; timing continuation, review/adoption, admitted-run retention, and unavailable-policy behavior are declared as `inventory4-C01`, `inventory4-C02`, `inventory4-C03`, and `inventory4-C04`.

## 6. Functional behavior and state model

### 6.1 Scheduled run lifecycle

A scheduled run is distinct from a manual invocation and is bound at admission to the household rule, installation/channel, enabled immutable rule revision, current grant, reviewed timing policy, control revision, trigger identity, and the snapshots needed to explain its result. The scheduler uses the existing shared rule model and engine; it must not simulate a manual trigger or create a human session. The optional stock condition is evaluated within its authorized scope. A false condition produces an inspectable no-task outcome; a true condition invokes the ordinary task-creation application use case and records its actual receipt. User-facing history distinguishes scheduled from manual invocation, task creation from a failed following read, and unavailable history from an empty history.

The rule owns a Domus non-human grant independently of the activating person. Grant management requires current automation-administration permission; a task-creation grant additionally requires current task-creation permission and verified household access. The grant delegates task creation and only necessary scoped condition reads. It never confers global administrator authority, stores credentials, or fabricates `HumanIdentity`. Before every admission and each new effect, the engine rechecks grant binding and capability, household scope, rule enablement, and current control. Revocation or disablement prevents new admissions/effects. An atomic command already started may finish, with its actual receipt and history retained.

Saving a draft leaves the grant unchanged. Explicitly switching the enabled revision retires the previous binding and requires deliberate grant creation for the new revision. A household, installation, or channel change that invalidates the binding prevents execution. Restart and restoration revalidate the persisted grant and binding, retain valid rule-owned identity, and preserve receipt and cursor provenance. They do not redeliver committed effects or automatically resume cancelled work.

### 6.2 Timing and trigger boundaries

Within one immutable rule revision, the durable nominal frontier includes dates omitted at startup and dates coalesced to one resolved instant. Enabling that revision again or adopting another policy cannot make those dates eligible again; planning starts strictly after both that frontier and the current instant.

Daily triggers use the reviewed pinned time zone and recurrence-resolution policy. A household timing change alone neither switches policy nor pauses an otherwise resolvable approved schedule. Adoption changes only future eligible trigger resolution. Already admitted runs retain original timing, snapshots, and outcomes; adoption does not rewrite prior work or replay missed or cancelled triggers. If the approved policy cannot be verified or resolved, the schedule exposes an unavailable or review state and never silently substitutes the proposed household policy.

At startup, daily times that became due while Domus was stopped and were not durably admitted are skipped. Persist a bounded, inspectable suppression/cursor fact so another worker or restart cannot reinterpret those times as new work; the next strictly future eligible time may proceed. This startup rule does not skip ordinary in-process worker delay. Define the startup cutoff and serialize its cursor decision with admission, disable/re-enable, and restoration so races cannot create duplicate or backfilled work. Already admitted work and committed receipts continue under the retained owner’s durability and disablement contracts.

The retained owner specifies immutable run snapshots, durable receipts, authorization before effects, and disablement that stops later steps while preserving an already-started atomic command. The new observable refinements are grant binding and revalidation (`inventory3-C01`, `inventory3-C02`, `inventory3-C03`, `inventory3-C04`, `inventory3-C05`) and timing-policy continuation, review, adoption, and unavailable states (`inventory4-C01`, `inventory4-C02`, `inventory4-C03`, `inventory4-C04`).

## 7. User-facing configuration

### 7.1 Rule enablement and grant

Enabling is an explicit reviewed action on one saved immutable Daily revision. The review identifies the rule and revision, household, installation/channel, narrow delegated capability, approved timing policy, and upcoming resolved times. The member confirms enablement deliberately; merely saving a draft or viewing a preview cannot create authority. Automation-administration permission is required to manage the grant, and task-creation permission plus verified household access are required to grant task creation. Permission and binding are current checks, not enable-time snapshots.

The grant is rule-owned and scoped to task creation plus necessary condition reads. It has no user-configurable expansion to administrator authority, credentials, fabricated human identity, or unrelated household data. Disablement prevents future admissions and effects; an already-started atomic task command may settle and its actual result remains visible. Re-enabling permits future work but does not resume previously cancelled runs.

### 7.2 Timing review and adoption

The schedule view presents the currently approved pinned time zone and recurrence-resolution policy alongside the proposed household policy, with upcoming resolved times and relevant spring-gap or repeated-time differences. An authorized member explicitly adopts the proposed policy by confirming the current rule/control revision and the reviewed policy. Without that confirmation, the approved policy continues while resolvable. If it is no longer verifiable or resolvable, show unavailable/review status rather than silently switching.

Timing configuration is snapshotted per admitted run. Adoption affects only future eligible triggers. The startup behavior skips unadmitted daily times missed while Domus was stopped, records a bounded suppression/cursor fact, and continues at the next strictly future time; it does not backfill missed dates. This choice does not make routine in-process worker delay a suppression condition.

## 8. Clean Architecture design

### 8.1 Responsibilities and dependency direction

The domain owns typed Daily rule validation, trigger identity, recurrence eligibility decisions, policy binding invariants, and deterministic suppression outcomes. It remains independent of scheduler, database, web, and provider details. The existing domain contract is in `backend/src/domus/domain/automation.py`; the existing recurrence preview boundary is visible in `backend/src/domus/application/automation_schedule_preview.py`.

Application use cases own reviewed enablement, grant lifecycle, policy review/adoption, due-trigger admission, condition evaluation, run advancement, disablement fencing, and bounded startup cursor handling. They call semantic ports for durable rule/control/grant state, schedule time resolution, atomic admission and receipts, and scoped condition reads. Scheduled task creation must use the ordinary authorized task-command path, not duplicate its validation or storage behavior; the existing application entrypoints are `backend/src/domus/application/task_commands.py` and `backend/src/domus/application/task_authorization.py`. The current manual automation implementation in `backend/src/domus/application/automation_execution.py` remains the manual path and must not be invoked with a manufactured human identity.

Adapters own persistence, transaction/lease mechanics, scheduler wakeups, time-provider mapping, and external error translation. Composition wires one scheduler into the shared automation engine. Entrypoints authenticate members for enablement, control, review, and history queries; workers carry the persisted rule grant and must revalidate it, not treat a queued message or caller-provided identity as authority. Presentation renders status and history from bounded permission-aware read models; it does not decide policy or mutate schedule state.

### 8.2 Durable state, concurrency, and trust

Persist the enabled immutable revision, rule-owned grant binding and capability, current control revision, pinned timing policy identity, eligible-time cursor, trigger identity, admission/run state, and actual task receipt/outcome. Admission and cursor movement must be duplicate-safe across concurrent workers, retries, lost replies, restart, and restore. Serialize disablement against admission and each new effect. Preserve already-started atomic commands and committed receipts; cancelled work is not automatically resumed. A draft save cannot mutate the enabled binding. Revision switch retires the old binding before a new deliberately reviewed grant can enable the replacement.

Persist startup suppression for missed, unadmitted daily times so recovery cannot reinterpret them. The startup cutoff must distinguish times due while the process was stopped from ordinary in-process delay, and coordinate with cursor advancement, worker races, disable/re-enable, and restoration. Revalidate current grant, household/installation/channel binding, enabled revision, and capability before admission and each effect. Invalid or unavailable policy/binding fails closed with an inspectable review/denial state, not an empty-history result or silent policy substitution.

### 8.3 Enforceable architecture constraints

Keep one engine and route task effects through the shared authorized use case, consistent with `docs/adr/0005-one-automation-engine.md` and the retained contract in `specs/household-automation.md`. Domain policy must remain pure; application owns orchestration and ports; adapters cannot redefine authorization or recurrence policy; presentation cannot perform mutations. The companion must define executable dependency, schema, grant-scope, receipt/idempotency, concurrency, restart, and restore contract checks before implementation. Exact installed project invocations and their verification scope remain unverified in this source review.

## 16. Acceptance scenarios

1. Given a deliberately reviewed daily revision, when an authorized member enables it, then Domus creates a rule-owned non-human grant bound to the installation/channel, household, rule, and enabled immutable revision, and exposes the enabled revision and reviewed timing policy.
2. Given a member who lacks current automation-administration permission, when they manage the grant, then the operation is denied; a task-creation grant also requires current task-creation permission and verified household access. Delegation remains limited to task creation and necessary scoped condition reads, without global administrator authority, stored credentials, or a fabricated HumanIdentity.
3. Given a grant whose current binding or capability has changed, when admission or an effect is attempted, then revalidation prevents it. Revocation or disablement prevents new effects while retaining the actual outcome and history of an already-started atomic command.
4. Given a saved draft or an explicit enabled-revision switch, when the rule changes, then a draft leaves the grant unchanged; switching retires the previous binding and execution waits for a deliberately created new grant. An invalidated household, installation, or channel binding prevents execution.
5. Given a restart or restoration, when persisted rule state is checked, then a valid current grant retains its rule-owned identity, invalid state is rejected, and receipt/cursor provenance is retained without redelivery or automatic resumption of cancelled work.
6. Given a household timing-policy change, when an otherwise resolvable approved schedule is due, then future execution continues under its reviewed pinned zone and recurrence-resolution policy until an authorized member explicitly adopts a newly reviewed policy.
7. Given the policy review view, when an authorized member compares approved and proposed policies, then both policies and upcoming resolved times are shown, including relevant gap and repeated-time differences; adoption confirms the current rule/control revision and newly reviewed policy.
8. Given an already admitted run when a policy is adopted, then its timing, snapshots, and outcome remain unchanged; only future eligible triggers use the adopted policy, with no replay of earlier, missed, or cancelled triggers.
9. Given an approved policy that cannot be verified or resolved, when scheduled status is shown, then the schedule is unavailable or requires review and does not silently substitute the proposed household policy.
